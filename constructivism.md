# Constructivism

## What it is

Constructivism was a modernist art and design movement that developed in Russia around the time of the Russian Revolution of 1917. Constructivist artists rejected traditional approaches to art and focused on creating work that had a practical or social purpose.

The style often uses strong geometric forms, bold typography, limited colors, and dramatic arrangements. Constructivist graphic design became especially recognizable through posters, advertisements, books, and other forms of visual communication.

## Recognize and apply it

Three visual features that can help identify Constructivism are:

1. **Geometric shapes and diagonal elements** — Circles, rectangles, triangles, and strong diagonal lines are often used to create movement and direct attention.
2. **Bold typography** — Large, heavy type is used as an important visual element rather than simply being used to provide information.
3. **Limited and high-contrast colors** — Red, black, white, and neutral colors are commonly combined to create bold contrast.

The style works well when a design needs to feel powerful, energetic, and direct. These features can be applied to posters and website hero sections by using strong typography, geometric organization, and clear visual hierarchy.

## Historical examples

### Example 1: Beat the Whites with the Red Wedge, 1920

- **Creator, title, date, institution:** El Lissitzky, *Beat the Whites with the Red Wedge*, 1920, Russian State Library, Moscow.
- **Direct collection/source link:** [MoMA — Engineer, Agitator, Constructor](https://www.moma.org/d/pdfs/W1siZiIsIjIwMjEvMDMvMTEvMWk1c3lsNHhxN19Nb01BX0VuZ2luZWVyQWdpdGF0b3JDb25zdHJ1Y3Rvcl9QUkVWSUVXLnBkZiJdXQ/MoMA_EngineerAgitatorConstructor_PREVIEW.pdf?sha=95229b2ce1947ac9)
- **Image or viewing link; credit and reuse terms:** [View the work through MoMA](https://www.moma.org/d/pdfs/W1siZiIsIjIwMjEvMDMvMTEvMWk1c3lsNHhxN19Nb01BX0VuZ2luZWVyQWdpdGF0b3JDb25zdHJ1Y3Rvcl9QUkVWSUVXLnBkZiJdXQ/MoMA_EngineerAgitatorConstructor_PREVIEW.pdf?sha=95229b2ce1947ac9). The work is identified by MoMA as part of the Russian State Library collection in Moscow.
- **What I observe:** The design uses simple geometric forms and a limited red, black, and white color palette. The large red wedge and diagonal composition create movement and make the design feel direct and powerful.
- **How I would adapt it in a hero:** I would use large geometric shapes and diagonal elements to direct attention toward the headline and CTA while keeping the color palette limited and high contrast.

### Example 2: Poster for Mossel'prom Cooking Oil, 1923

- **Creator, title, date, institution:** Aleksandr Rodchenko, Reklam-Konstruktor Agency, *Poster for Mossel'prom Cooking Oil*, 1923, Museum of Modern Art (MoMA).
- **Direct collection/source link:** [MoMA — Poster for Mossel'prom Cooking Oil](https://www.moma.org/collection/works/280249)
- **Image or viewing link; credit and reuse terms:** [View the work at MoMA](https://www.moma.org/collection/works/280249). Credit: The Merrill C. Berman Collection. Follow MoMA's licensing guidance before reproducing the image.
- **What I observe:** The poster uses bold typography, geometric organization, strong contrast, and a limited color palette. The text and shapes work together as visual elements rather than keeping the typography separate from the overall composition.
- **How I would adapt it in a hero:** I would use oversized typography with geometric shapes surrounding the headline and CTA to create a bold design that immediately draws attention to the main message.
