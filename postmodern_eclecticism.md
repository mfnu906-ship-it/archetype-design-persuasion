# Postmodern Eclecticism

[Home](README.md)

## What it is

Postmodern Eclecticism is a design approach associated with Postmodernism that became especially influential during the 1970s and 1980s. Unlike Modernism, which often emphasized simplicity, order, and function, Postmodern design allowed designers to mix different styles, historical references, decorative elements, colors, patterns, and visual ideas.

The eclectic approach does not require every element to follow one consistent visual system. Instead, designers can combine influences from different periods and styles to create work that feels expressive, playful, and unexpected.

## Recognize and apply it

Three visual features that can help identify Postmodern Eclecticism are:

1. **Mixing different styles** — Designs may combine historical references, contemporary graphics, decorative elements, and different visual influences within one composition.
2. **Bold colors and patterns** — Bright colors, decorative patterns, geometric forms, and unusual combinations can make the design feel energetic and expressive.
3. **Playful and unconventional layouts** — Text, images, shapes, and other elements may be arranged in unexpected ways rather than following a strict Modernist grid.

The style works well when a design should feel expressive, energetic, playful, or visually distinctive. In a hero design, these characteristics can be used to attract attention while still keeping the main message and CTA understandable.

## Historical examples

### Example 1: Carlton Room Divider, 1981

- **Creator, title, date, institution:** Ettore Sottsass, *Carlton Room Divider*, 1981, Museum of Modern Art (MoMA).
- **Direct collection/source link:** [MoMA — Carlton Room Divider](https://www.moma.org/collection/works/1856)
- **Image or viewing link; credit and reuse terms:** [View the work at MoMA](https://www.moma.org/collection/works/1856). Credit and reproduction information are provided by the Museum of Modern Art.
- **What I observe:** The design combines bold colors, geometric forms, unusual proportions, and decorative elements. Instead of emphasizing simplicity, the object uses unexpected combinations to create a playful and expressive appearance.
- **How I would adapt it in a hero:** I would combine bold geometric shapes, contrasting colors, and playful placement of visual elements while keeping the headline and CTA clearly visible.

### Example 2: Casablanca Sideboard, 1981

- **Creator, title, date, institution:** Ettore Sottsass, *Casablanca Sideboard*, 1981, Philadelphia Museum of Art.
- **Direct collection/source link:** [Philadelphia Museum of Art — Casablanca](https://philamuseum.org/collection/object/104668)
- **Image or viewing link; credit and reuse terms:** [View the work at the Philadelphia Museum of Art](https://philamuseum.org/collection/object/104668). Follow the museum's image-use and copyright guidance before reproducing the image.
- **What I observe:** The piece uses strong geometric forms, contrasting colors, patterns, and an unconventional overall structure. The different elements create a visually energetic design rather than a restrained or minimal composition.
- **How I would adapt it in a hero:** I would use contrasting shapes, patterns, and varied visual elements to make the hero feel energetic and distinctive while maintaining enough organization for the audience to understand the message.
