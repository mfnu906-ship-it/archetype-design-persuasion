# Scarcity

[Home](README.md)

## What it is

Scarcity is a persuasion principle based on the idea that people often place more value on something when it is limited or difficult to obtain. When an opportunity, product, or resource appears to be running out, people may feel a stronger desire to act before they lose the opportunity.

Scarcity can be based on limited quantities, limited time, exclusive access, or unique opportunities. It creates urgency by making the audience aware that waiting could mean missing out.

## Recognize and apply it

Scarcity can be recognized when a message emphasizes limited availability, exclusivity, deadlines, or the possibility of missing an opportunity. Words and phrases such as “limited,” “only a few left,” “exclusive,” “while supplies last,” and “ends soon” can create this feeling.

In design and communication, Scarcity can be applied by:

- Highlighting limited quantities of a product.
- Creating limited-edition products or collections.
- Using deadlines for offers or opportunities.
- Offering exclusive access to certain products or experiences.
- Clearly communicating when an opportunity will no longer be available.

## Example 1: Nike

Nike releases certain sneakers and collections in limited quantities. Limited releases can increase demand because customers know that the product may become difficult to purchase once the available inventory is gone.

**How Scarcity is used:** Limited availability gives customers a reason to make a purchasing decision sooner rather than waiting.

**Source:** [Nike](https://www.nike.com/)

## Example 2: Supreme

Supreme is known for releasing products through limited drops rather than keeping every product continuously available. This can create urgency because customers know that popular items may sell out and may not be easily available again.

**How Scarcity is used:** Limited product drops make availability part of the purchasing decision and encourage customers to act before an item sells out.

**Source:** [Supreme](https://supreme.com/)

## How I would use Scarcity

I would use Scarcity by clearly showing the audience that a product or opportunity has limited availability without making the message difficult to understand. I would use limited quantities, exclusive releases, or time-sensitive offers to give the audience a reason to act sooner.

For the Outlaw archetype, Scarcity works especially well because limited-edition products can reinforce individuality and exclusivity. A limited streetwear collection, for example, can make the audience feel that they are purchasing something that not everyone will have.
